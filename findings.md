# Findings

_Generated by `src/02_analysis.py`. Revenue is indexed, never absolute._

## 1. Booking channel

Online bookings carry a **32% higher average value** than staff-assisted (index 132 vs 100) and larger parties (3.35 vs 2.93 pax).
Staff-assisted bookings are overwhelmingly walk-ins: median lead time 0.01 days vs 1.99 for online.

## 2. Site comparison

Cambridge takes 74% of bookings online; Watford only 54%.
Watford is therefore far more walk-in dependent (46% vs 26%), and skews toward the lower-value channel.
**Opportunity:** shifting Watford's online share to Cambridge's level would move volume into a channel worth 32% more per booking.

## 3. Booking lead time

**53% of bookings are made on the day of the session**, and 75% within a week.
This is the single most actionable finding: promotional lead times measured in weeks are aimed at a minority of demand. Same-week and same-day activity is where the volume is.

## 4. Demand trend and forecast

Full months analysed: 14 (partial first/last months excluded).
Naive last-month forecast: **MAPE 18.2%**. 3-month moving average: **MAPE 14.5%**.
The moving average performs better, giving a documented benchmark any future model must beat.

## 5. What predicts a repeat booking

Overall repeat rate: **11.7%** of customers book again (217 of 1,856).
By channel: Online 11.2% vs Staff-assisted 12.7% - z=-0.94, p=0.35, **not statistically significant**.
By site: Cambridge 12.0% vs Watford 11.4% - z=0.40, p=0.69, **not statistically significant**.

The apparent differences in repeat rate by channel and site do not survive a two-proportion z-test at n=1,856. They are reported here as null results rather than dropped, because a difference that fails significance testing is a finding: it says repeat behaviour is not driven by where or how the customer was acquired.

The 32% revenue premium for online bookings, by contrast, is highly significant (Welch t=7.6, p<0.0001, n=1,386 vs 760) - it is the one channel difference solid enough to act on.
