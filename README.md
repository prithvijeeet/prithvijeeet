### What I'm working on

**[VRXtra Booking Analytics](https://github.com/prithvijeeet/vrxtra-booking-analytics)** ‚Äî Management believed there was a weekday slump and a weekend rush, but nobody had tested it. I analysed 2,135 bookings across two UK VR venues and found the assumption was right about the pattern and wrong about the cause.

Using school holidays as a natural experiment ‚Äî same venue, same prices, same marketing, no children in school ‚Äî midweek demand rises **3.2√ó** on its own, closing roughly **91%** of the weekday gap. The slump is largely a structural constraint the business doesn't control, not a demand problem, which changes what's worth spending money on.

Along the way: a 32% online booking premium that held up under a Welch t-test (p < 0.0001), a retention difference that *didn't* survive significance testing and got published as a null result, and an Excel scenario model built specifically to stop the headline recommendation being oversold.

### How I work

- **Test the assumption before accepting it.** The most useful finding in that project contradicted the brief I was given.
- **Check significance before reporting.** Two of my apparent findings turned out to be noise. Saying so is more useful than quietly dropping them.
- **Count money, not rows.** Segmenting by booking volume and by revenue gave opposite answers. Only one of them should drive spend.
- **Handle data properly.** Personal data pseudonymised before analysis, raw exports never committed, published figures indexed rather than absolute.
